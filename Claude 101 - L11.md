4. **My own ideas**: how to try them (suited to my background)

(My own suggestions, not from the lesson.)

**Try 1**: Prepare for a sales deal (from the Sales list)

"I'm preparing for a call with [type of customer]. Here's what I know: [facts]. Help me list likely questions, objections, and talking points. Ask me what's missing first. Keep it to one page."

**Try 2**: Adapt content across platforms (from the Marketing list)

"Here's a post I wrote: [paste]. Rewrite it as (1) a short LinkedIn post, (2) a 3-line message, and (3) a friendly email. Keep the same key message and a professional but warm tone."

**Try 3**: Analyze patterns in feedback (from the General list)

"Here are 20 customer comments: [paste or upload]. Group them into themes, count how often each appears, and tell me the top 3 problems. Show one example quote for each."

**Try 4** (my own, for my career goal): a resume helper

"I'm moving from inside sales (resume-writing services) into AI security. Turn these 5 bullet points into a clear, plain-language resume summary. Under 80 words. Flag anything vague that I should make more specific."

Check-the-answer habit for every try (Discernment)
 Are the facts true? (Claude can sound sure and be wrong.)
 Is the tone right?
 Did I share anything private that I shouldn't have?

**Link to my goals**
Everyday use: my best starting points are the Sales use cases and the resume and writing ideas, since they match my experience.
AI Security (later): every use case involves giving Claude real data (customer comments, pipeline numbers, financial models, legal documents). That's where data privacy (Diligence) and checking outputs (Discernment) matter most.
Legal and finance use cases especially involve confidential data.
"Verify statistics from raw data" is a good reminder: AI can be confidently wrong, so I check against the source.
A Skill that packages brand guidelines is useful, but remember the Lesson 7 rule: only use trusted Skills and review outside ones.
